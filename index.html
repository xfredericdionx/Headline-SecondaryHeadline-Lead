The Anatomy of an Article

Before you dive into the facts, every news article relies on a structured opening to hook the reader. Let's break down the three most crucial parts of a news intro.

1. Headline

The Hook

The main title of the article. It must be punchy, short, and use active verbs. Its main job is to grab the reader's attention and summarize the core event.

"City Council Bans Single-Use Plastics"

2. Secondary Headline

The Bridge / The Deck

Located just below the main headline. It adds vital context or a crucial secondary detail, acting as a bridge to keep you reading before the actual article starts.

"Local businesses are given a six-month grace period to transition to greener alternatives."

3. Lead (Lede)

The Foundation

The very first paragraph of the article. It contains the most important information and usually answers the 5 Ws: Who, What, Where, When, and Why.

"In a unanimous vote Thursday evening, city officials passed a strict new bylaw prohibiting the distribution of plastic bags to combat local pollution."
